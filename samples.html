EXAMPLE REQUEST · GATE POLY-SI DRY ETCH

Give us the process target — get a tool proposal back

300 mm gate poly-Si dry etch on an ICP source with wafer bias. All we received were targets for etch rate, selectivity, non-uniformity and pressure, plus the gases, bias frequencies and chuck. We computed three source structures against two bias frequencies (13.56 and 2 MHz) and picked the recommended structure and recipe from the 193 operating conditions that met every target. About one day from request to proposal. Below is the proposal's first page.

What the proposal contains

  • Three source structures compared — type · chamber · window · coils
  • Source matcher + splitter, a bias matcher and matching window per bias frequency
  • The recommended recipe, and one per structure and frequency
  • Process window in pressure · source power · bias power
  • Anything we did not compute is marked "not met" or "out of prediction scope"

All values are predictions before installation. Chuck heating was not part of this design and is marked not met; CD, profile and production items are outside the prediction scope. This is an example request made to show the method, not a real order.

REQUEST · RF MATCHING

From the plasma to the matcher spec

The same plasma calculation now gives the matcher-output impedance over the whole operating window, the matching window on a Smith chart, a wiring guide and a matcher specification — capacitor ranges, ratings, heat and efficiency — stated as ranges with their error bands. Power split between coils and matcher loss feed back into the plasma until they agree.

What we quote in proposals

Coil current (same supplied power)±10 %
Matcher-output impedance (cylindrical solenoid, low-pressure N₂)±15 %
Matcher position (fixed component values)±12 %p
Matcher efficiency (estimate)±5 %p
Component ratings (kV · A rms)design range

Reference basis: blind comparison with real cylindrical tools, no tool-specific tuning — coil current ±2–5 %, matcher-output impedance ±11 % (some configurations).
